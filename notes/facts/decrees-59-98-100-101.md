## What the sources say

SANA published all three, dated **9 May 2026**, signed by al-Sharaa. The Arabic
texts are archived by Syrian Memory.

- **Decree 98 of 2026** — appoints **Abdulrahman Badr al-Deen al-A' ma** as
  **Secretary-General of the Presidency**. SANA: "Damascus, May 9 (SANA) President
  Ahmad al-Sharaa issued Decree No. 98 of 2026 appointing Abdulrahman Badr
  al-Deen al-A'ma as Secretary-General of the Presidency of the Republic."
  He was previously **Governor of Homs** and a former Minister of Development and
  Humanitarian Affairs. He **replaced Maher al-Sharaa**, the president's brother —
  Al Jazeera reports the move "appeared to be a response to accusations of
  nepotism", while also noting "the new appointees come overwhelmingly from his
  trusted network".
- **Decree 100 of 2026** — Article 1: `يُعين السيد خالد فواز زعرور وزيراً للإعلام`
  (Khaled Fawaz Zaarour appointed Minister of Information). He **replaced Hamza
  Mustafa**, who moved to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Zaarour was Dean of the
  Faculty of Media at Damascus University from March 2025.
- **Decree 101 of 2026** — Article 1: `يُعين السيد باسل حافظ السودان وزيراً للزراعة`
  (Basel Hafez al-Suwaidan appointed Minister of Agriculture), previously Deputy
  Minister. Al Jazeera notes the new agriculture minister is the **defence
  minister's cousin**.

The same night also issued **gubernatorial appointments for Homs, Quneitra, Latakia
and Deir ez-Zor** (Decree 105 appointed Ziad Fawaz al-Ayash as Governor of Deir
ez-Zor). Al Jazeera headlined it as "**Syria's first government reshuffle since
al-Assad's ouster**".

## A significant detail the game does not model

**Basel Hafez al-Suwaidan — the Minister of Agriculture appointed by Decree 101 —
is the same person who heads the Anti-Illicit Enrichment Commission's settlements
work.** Enab Baladi reports "Head of Syria's Anti-Illicit Enrichment Commission
Basel Hafez al-Suwaidan announces a six month voluntary disclosure program,
January 28, 2026", and Al Jazeera describes him as heading "a committee tasked with
reaching settlements with business tycoons linked to the Assad-era elite" while
taking the agriculture portfolio.

**One man holding both the agriculture ministry and the asset-recovery portfolio**
is a very playable concentration of power, and the game separates them.

The settlements programme itself:
- Six-month **voluntary disclosure window**, announced 28 January 2026, expiring
  end of May 2026.
- **50 settlement requests** to be decided by end of May 2026.
- **Security and army personnel of the ousted regime are not eligible**; their
  files go to the judiciary for indictment and precautionary measures against
  family assets.
- Settled properties may be disclosed under a transparency principle.
- After the window closes, all files go **exclusively** to the judiciary.

## Decree 13 of 2026 — a different decree entirely

The fact-check report claimed "**Decision/Decree No. 13 of May 4, 2025**: National
Committee for Combating Illicit Enrichment". That is wrong on subject matter.

**Decree No. 13 of 2026** (16 January 2026) is about **Kurdish citizenship**:
- Article 1: "Syrian Kurdish citizens are considered an essential and authentic
  part of the Syrian people, and their cultural and linguistic identity is an
  inseparable part of Syria's unified and diverse national identity."
- Abolishes all laws and exceptional measures arising from the **1962 Hasakah
  census**.
- Grants Syrian nationality to Kurdish-origin residents, including those previously
  unregistered.
- Designates **Nowruz Day (21 March)** a paid official holiday nationwide.
- Article 8: in force on issuance.

SANA's article on it is headlined "Syria's historic decree" and framed as ending
decades of marginalisation. Decree numbers are year-scoped, so "Decree 13 of 2025"
and "Decree 13 of 2026" are entirely different instruments.

The Anti-Illicit Enrichment Commission **does exist** — al-Suwaidan heads it — but
**no Decree 13 of 2025 creating it was located**, and the report's citation for it
was the absent design document. See
[decrees-13-16-illicit-wealth.md](decrees-13-16-illicit-wealth.md).

## Verdict

- **Decrees 98, 100, 101: VERIFIED, and the game is correct on all three**, down
  to the spelling of al-Suwaidan's name.
- **Decree 13 of 2025 (illicit enrichment): REFUTED as stated.** The real Decree
  13 of 2026 concerns Kurdish citizenship.
- **Decree 59 of 10 March 2026: still UNVERIFIED.** A High Committee for
  Infrastructure Rehabilitation, reportedly chaired by the emergency minister with
  Finance, Housing, Social Affairs, Local Administration and the Governors of
  Aleppo, Hama and Idlib. Not found. The report's citation was `sfuturem.org`.

## Game impact

- **Nothing to correct** on 98/100/101. The code is accurate.
- **MISSING: the nepotism dimension.** Maher al-Sharaa (the president's brother)
  held the presidency secretariat until May 2026, and the reshuffle that removed
  him was publicly read as a response to nepotism criticism while simultaneously
  installing people from the president's own network. That is a well-shaped
  political-capital mechanic and the game has no equivalent.
- **MISSING: al-Suwaidan's dual portfolio.** Agriculture policy and asset
  recovery in the same hands, with a live six-month settlement window. The game
  separates agriculture into a ministry and confiscation into a player decision.
- **MISSING: the Kurdish citizenship question entirely.** Decree 13 of 2026
  abolishes the 1962 Hasakah census, regularises unregistered Kurds and makes
  Nowruz a paid holiday. Any Syrian president inherits this as a foundational
  citizenship-and-registration decision. The game has no such axis, and no
  demographic modelling beyond population totals.
- **MISSING: settlement deadlines.** A six-month window that closes and sends
  everything to the judiciary is a clean time pressure mechanic.
- **NOT MODELLED: gubernatorial appointments.** Four governors appointed in one
  night is a patronage lever.
